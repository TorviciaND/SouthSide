Our promises

What we commit to our community.

A place to connect

We are intentionally fostering an expanding circle of deep, interpersonal relationships.

A space to grow

We are deepening our worship — honoring familiar traditions while expanding into new, meaningful experiences.

A safe haven

We are dedicated to maintaining our building and grounds as a safe, welcoming place for all to gather.

We are expanding our Alverna Covenant and Green Chalice initiatives, treating our property as a microcosm of the ecosphere.

We are broadening our digital impact to become a regional resource for leadership formation and congregational vitality.

Symbols & values

Our building speaks.

Inclusive, affirming, multi-voiced, gifts-based, advocating peace and justice for all — through our symbols we represent our deepest values.

The Circle photo

The Circle

Moving away from traditional rows, our circular building and diverse logo represent complete inclusion — welcoming all races, abilities, gender identities, and sexual orientations into our community.

The Chalice photo

The Chalice

As the center of our worship, the chalice represents communion and our connection to the broader Disciples of Christ — a radical welcome to Christ's table where everyone is embraced without exception.

The Tables photo

The Tables

Our central communion table rests on a globe, symbolizing God's welcome to the entire world. Our fellowship table is draped in a signed tapestry — a gathering place where we light candles and pray for global peace.

The Crosses photo

The Crosses

From the illuminated cross representing Jesus' sacrificial values to the straw cross offering a safe, healing embrace — our crosses remind us we are called to follow the Jesus way and provide sanctuary to all.

The Stole photo

The Stole

Belonging to the late Pastor Bruce Naylor, this stole symbolizes servant leadership. Like Jesus washing his disciples' feet, our leaders serve by offering their God-given gifts rather than asserting superiority.

Wholeness photo

Wholeness out of brokenness

This powerful symbol illustrates how God creates beauty from our brokenness — our commitment to the transformational work of changing lives and systems to bring wholeness to a fractured world.

The Windows photo

The Windows

These historic windows acknowledge the generations of faithful people who paved the way for us — reminding us we are part of an ongoing journey connected to a rich history of dreamers and disciples.

The Shutters photo

The Shutters

Painted in rainbow colors, these shutters declare our core values of justice, equity, and inclusion — actively proclaiming our commitment to welcome refugees, immigrants, and all marginalized communities.

FAQ

Questions we hear often.

Worship & welcome

Is there a dress code? ▾
No. Some members wear jeans and casual wear, others may dress up. You are welcome to wear whatever you are most comfortable in.
Are children welcome? ▾
Yes. Our Sunday services are intergenerational. Children are welcome to participate fully. We also provide "church grandparents" to assist parents so they can participate fully in the service.
Are musical gifts welcome? ▾
Yes. During service we use the New Century Hymnal. Our hymns often include drums — you are welcome to join us in drumming!
Do you perform weddings for all couples? ▾
Yes. Our pastors are inclusive and offer marriage to all who request it and complete our preparation process.

Beliefs & affiliation

Do you follow a creed? ▾
No. As Disciples of Christ, we have no creed. Our faith is centered on the message of Jesus.
Is communion open to everyone? ▾
Yes. There are no restrictions at our table. Everyone is invited — you do not need to be a member, believe a certain way, or have attended before to receive communion.
Why is there no flag in the sanctuary? ▾
We do not display a US flag because we pledge our ultimate allegiance to the realm of God rather than a nation.
Are you part of a denomination? ▾
We are affiliated with the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ) and the Indiana Region. Our congregation is locally governed and independent in its decision-making.
